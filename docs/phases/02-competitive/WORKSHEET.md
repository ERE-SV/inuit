## Learn (read before working)

### What this phase is

You tear down who already helps Swiss employers hire and Swiss seekers find jobs — both **direct** rivals (same job) and **indirect** ones (different product that still solves “get candidates / get a job”). You fill a matrix with sourced facts, mark pricing `unknown` unless you can prove it, and write a short **white space** note: what gap our matchmaking idea could own. You are not locking a brand slogan yet.

### Key terms

| Term | Meaning in plain language | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Competitive analysis** | Structured comparison of rivals: who they serve, features, pricing (if known), GTM, strengths/weaknesses — backed by evidence | Stops “I feel LinkedIn is big” from becoming the whole strategy |
| **Positioning** | The clear place *we* want to own in the customer’s mind vs alternatives (“for X who hate Y, we are Z”) | Different from listing competitors; it is the *choice* of contrast |
| **Direct competitor** | Same core job: help fill roles / find roles via listings or matching | Must be in the matrix (≥3) |
| **Indirect competitor** | Different product, same budget or habit (agencies, ATS, employer-brand sites, aggregators) | Often where money and workflow already sit (≥3) |
| **White space** | A real gap rivals leave open that evidence supports | Feeds beachhead + messaging; must not be wishful |
| **Feature / GTM comparison** | Side-by-side on product capabilities *and* how they acquire customers | Matching vs listing is a product *and* go-to-market difference |

### Competitive analysis vs positioning (beginner)

1. **First analyze:** fill the matrix — facts about others.  
2. **Then position:** from white space + beachhead (Phase 03), draft how *we* differ.  
3. Do not invent a tagline before the matrix. Do not claim “we are 10× cheaper” without sourced competitor prices.

### Common beginner mistakes

- Fewer than 8 competitors, or only big portals  
- Inventing CHF prices (brief’s 2–3k CHF is an estimate to **validate**, not a fact)  
- Confusing Kununu/Glassdoor (brand/reviews) with a job board without saying so  
- “White space = AI matching” with no evidence rivals fail on noise, commute, or structured requirements  
- Mixing analysis (facts) with positioning (our claim) in one blurry paragraph
