## 1. Definition

You produce a **sourced picture of how hiring actually works in Switzerland today** — not only a list of job boards.

That means you can explain, with proof or honest `unknown`:

- **Volume and shape** — how many positions get filled; which markets are growing, seasonal, or tight vs slack.
- **Channels** — where KMU and large corporates look; which channel is dominant; what those channels cost and how they make money.
- **Company journey** — how a talent need is recognized; who hires; who decides; how that differs by company size.
- **Applicant journey** — how people find and choose employers; how many applications; role of salary and culture; why they leave; fluctuation by industry.
- **Pain** — what hurts each side, **where** in the journey, including pain you felt by applying yourself.
- **Institutions** — universities, Bund, cantons, subsidies.
- **Roles** — what is in high demand; which jobs are homogeneous vs heterogeneous; where a wrong hire is especially expensive.
- **Motion** — first-pass trends, and who is trying to disrupt this (including outside Europe).

This is **observation**. You are not locking a beachhead, a crawl plan, a brand, or a price.

**Done when:** every worksheet sub-question `01-T1-Q1` … `Q21` is answered with proof or `unknown`, the required tables exist, and the apply log is filled.

## 2. Why this task exists

Later phases choose a wedge (niche × geography), then GTM and product. They cannot do that from a channel list alone. They need to know **how hiring is done**, **who pays**, **where it hurts**, and **which numbers are unknown**.

If this is thin:

- Phase 02 compares competitors without knowing the buying journey or who is dominant.
- Phase 03 scores beachheads without fill volume, time-to-hire, fluctuation, or cost-of-wrong-hire.
- Phase 04 invents an employer motion (HR vs CEO vs line manager) instead of observing it.
- Pain in the brief (flood of applications, 40–50 applies, 2–3k CHF listings) stays a story.

T1 does **not** lock a niche. It locks a shared picture of the field.

## 4. Guiding context

### What you are mapping

Switzerland’s hiring market is not one portal. Typical pieces:

| Piece | Examples (starting points — verify) |
| --- | --- |
| Commercial boards / aggregators | jobs.ch and JobCloud brands, Indeed CH, regional boards |
| Networks | LinkedIn, XING |
| Direct | Company career pages, referrals, agencies |
| Public | arbeit.swiss, **RAV** (cantonal), SECO / Bund labour-market instruments |
| Education | University / FH career centers, apprenticeships (Lehrstellen) |

**KMU** (SMEs) and large corporates often do **not** use the same mix. A 20-person GmbH may use one paid listing + network; a bank may have TA, ATS, agencies, and a career site. Size is a first-class split in Q7 and Q12.

### Jobs-to-be-done (still required for channels)

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| “jobs.ch is the biggest Swiss job portal.” | “Seekers use it to browse a large CH inventory and apply with a stored profile; employers buy listings (and extras) to generate inbound applications.” |

### Brief claims = hypotheses

| Claim in the brief | What T1 should do |
| --- | --- |
| Listings ~**2–3k CHF per position** | Public price **or** `unknown`. Never paste the brief as a source. |
| Graduates **40–50 applications** / **3–6 months** | Source or `unknown` (Q14). |
| Multi-channel posting → cost and noise | Facts + labeled **inference** (Q5, Q15). |
| Cover letters are AI noise | Source or weak (Q15 / T2). |
| High cost of a bad hire in some niches | Q18 — sourced or `unknown`; do not invent CHF. |

### Homogeneous vs heterogeneous roles

- **Homogeneous:** many similar seats; requirements vary little (some volume nursing bands, some retail, some ops). Matching is simpler; differentiation may be location/shift/pay.
- **Heterogeneous:** roles differ a lot (specialized IT, consulting, some banking seats). Culture, stack, deal type, language mix matter. Structured matching is more valuable — **if** you can evidence the heterogeneity.

### CH vs the world

Start in **Switzerland**. DACH channels only if Swiss users already use them. Disruptors **outside Europe** belong in Q21 as *analogues* (what job they do, whether a CH equivalent exists) — not as “our market is the US.”

## 8. Sub-questions — what “complete” means

### Channels — Q1–Q5

**Q1.** Name + URL of every starter (and extra) channel you opened. If you skipped a starter row, one line why.

**Q2.** Seeker job vs employer job for jobs.ch, Indeed CH, LinkedIn, + two extras. Match the table. “Biggest” is not a job.

**Q3.** Public employer pricing per those portals: number + what it buys + URL + date, or `unknown`. No “around 2–3k.”

**Q4.** Career pages **and** arbeit.swiss / RAV: when used *instead of* a big portal.

**Q5.** 3–5 bullets: multi-channel cost or noise. Each bullet = fact → **inference**.

### Volume and market shape — Q6–Q11

**Q6. How many positions get filled?** CH figure for a named period (hires, filled vacancies, or the official series you found). Say what the statistic *is*. Industry split if published; else `unknown` by industry.

**Q7. KMU vs large corporates — where do they look?** Which channels, and how the mix differs. Observation + any sourced survey. **Inference** labeled.

**Q8. Time to hire by industry.** Sourced days/weeks or `unknown`. At least the three industries in §6.2.

**Q9. Cost per hire by industry.** Sourced or `unknown`. Separate this from **listing price** (Q3) and **channel cost per hire** (Q19). If a source mixes them, say so.

**Q10. High vs low talent competition.** Which occupations/industries look tight vs slack (vacancies vs unemployment, shortage lists, vacancy rates). ≥2 tight and ≥1 slack, or `unknown` with what you opened.

**Q11. Seasonal and growing markets.** What hiring is seasonal in CH (tourism, construction, graduates, public-sector cycles, etc.) — sourced. What is growing/shrinking (employment or vacancies by sector).

### Company journey — Q12

**Q12.** For **KMU** and **large corporate**, answer all four: (1) how a talent need is recognized; (2) who **hires** (who runs the process); (3) who **decides** (who says yes); (4) how those roles differ by size. Desk sources + what you saw on career/HR pages. Interviews optional and anecdotal.

### Institutions — Q13

**Q13.** Four short notes, each with a source or `unknown`:

- **Universities / FH / Berufsbildung** — career centers, fairs, Lehrstellen, alumni.
- **Bund** — SECO, unemployment insurance (ALV), arbeit.swiss, work-permit/immigration *only as it affects hiring*.
- **Cantons** — RAV and any cantonal labour or promotion role.
- **Subsidies** — public money that changes hiring (ALV, training, integration, disability, cantonal programs). Name the instrument; do not invent amounts.

### Applicant journey — Q14–Q16

**Q14.** Applicant journey in CH: how they **identify** possible companies; how they **decide**; role of **salary** and **culture**; **how many applications** you can source (brief’s 40–50 is a hypothesis). Point at the journey diagram.

**Q15.** Pain points for **employers** and **seekers**. Put each pain **on a journey step** (e.g. “screening,” “cover letter,” “ghosting after apply”). Facts + **inference**. Include where *you* felt it (Q16).

**Q16. First-person apply.** Complete the [apply log](../evidence/APPLY-LOG.md) for **≥3 companies**, **≥3 channel types** (e.g. jobs.ch or Indeed, a career page, LinkedIn/XING). Rules:

- Use **your own identity** and truthful information. No fake names, fake degrees, or fake current-employer stories.
- Submit only where you are a **plausible, honest** candidate. Otherwise stop at the last review screen and write why you did not submit.
- Include **one KMU and one larger employer** if you can find honest targets; if not, say why in the log.
- Log time, required fields, cover letter, account/ATS friction, and the pain-point IDs you will use in Q15.

### Leavers and roles — Q17–Q18

**Q17.** Why people leave (sourced reasons: pay, culture, commute, management, etc.) and **fluctuation / turnover by industry** if published. `unknown` by industry is allowed.

**Q18.** Which positions are in **high demand**; which families are **homogeneous vs heterogeneous** (your definition in §4 + evidence); where **cost of a wrong hire** looks extra high (seniority, regulated, revenue role). ≥4 families. Not a beachhead pick.

### Economics, trends, disruptors — Q19–Q21

**Q19.** Dominant channel (evidence, not vibe); what channels **cost** (list price and/or cost-per-hire); **how they make money**; **market or revenue share** if public (JobCloud filings, association estimates). Most share cells will be `unknown` — that is fine.

**Q20.** First-pass **trends** in the CH hiring market (≤6 bullets, each sourced). [01-T2](01-T2-TRENDS.md) reuses these and adds product “so what.” Do not skip T2.

**Q21.** Disrupting players / startups that change matching, apply, or talent marketplace — **at least two outside Europe** (required). For each: name, country, one-line job, URL, whether a CH/DACH equivalent exists. Phase 02 builds the matrix from this seed list.

## 11. Common mistakes

- Stopping at a portals table and calling the market “mapped.”
- Treating KMU and UBS-scale hiring as the same journey.
- Using listing *price* as cost *per hire*.
- Putting all pain in a blob instead of on journey steps.
- Skipping Bund/canton/RAV because the product idea feels private-sector.
- Writing Q21 as a global startup dump with no “job they do.”
- Completing T2 trends without a T1 inventory (or the reverse: a T1 trend list and skipping T2 so-what).
