## 1. Definition

You collect **at least four labour-market trend claims** that matter to *this* product (a Swiss two-sided matchmaking marketplace), and you give each claim a product-relevant **“so what.”**

A trend here is not “HR is changing.” It is a **checkable claim** about how hiring or job search is moving — official stats, apply-volume / AI-application evidence, skills-based or structured requirements, CH salary transparency — plus one extra trend you judge important.

**Done when:** at least four trend claims each have a source *and* a “so what” for matchmaking / less-noise hiring, and all five worksheet sub-questions are answered.

You are still **not** picking a niche.

---

## 2. Why this task exists

Channels (T1) say *where* hiring happens. Trends say *what is changing* so later positioning and beachhead criteria are not frozen in a 2019 job-board story.

If this is thin:

- Phase 03 “pain” scores become vibes (“everyone hates applications”).
- Phase 05 / 02 white space becomes “we use AI” with no evidence that noise, structure, or salary opacity is actually moving.
- Product work (commute, structured requirements, salary floors) has no market reason to exist.

The **“so what”** is the point. A sourced stat with no implication is trivia. An implication with no source is a pitch.

## 4. Guiding context

### What “so what” means here

One sentence: **what this fact means for a bidirectional matching product** — fewer unqualified applications, structured requirements (not keyword search alone), seeker salary floors, commute/preferences, or trust/transparency. Not “so we should raise funding.”

### Brief claims to treat as hypotheses

| Claim in the brief | What T2 should do |
| --- | --- |
| Graduates send **40–50 applications** and take **3–6 months** | Look for sourced evidence; if weak, **say so**. Do not repeat the brief as a fact. |
| Cover letters / applications are **AI-generated noise** | Q2: what you can actually source; quality of evidence, not a rant. |
| Portals deliver **unqualified inbound** | Related to Q2; keep company-side and seeker-side evidence distinct. |
| Matching should use **structured requirements**, not only ad text | Q3: what is changing in skills-based / structured hiring, so what for bidirectional match. |
| Company **salary ranges** and seeker **salary floors** matter | Q4: what CH transparency / benchmarks you can source; so what for profiles. |

### Official vs press

Q1 must lean **Tier A** (BFS, opendata.swiss, comparable official). Press is fine for Q2–Q5 if you label tier and do not build a gate-critical number on a blog. Sources older than 24 months: flag `dated` ([RESEARCH-STANDARD.md](../../../../RESEARCH-STANDARD.md)).

### CH first

Swiss official stats are the default. EU/DACH figures are supporting context only — say when they are not CH.

## 5. Scope

**In**

- Employment / unemployment / sector hiring facts relevant to a CH job marketplace (≤5 bullets for Q1).
- Sourced evidence on apply volume, unqualified applications, AI applications / cover letters (or an honest “weak”).
- Skills-based hiring or structured requirements → so what for matching.
- CH salary transparency or benchmarking → so what for company profiles and seeker floors.
- One extra trend *you* judge important for this product, with “what if this trend is wrong.”

**Out**

- A general “future of work” essay.
- Beachhead recommendation.
- Invented shortage percentages.
- Treating a single LinkedIn post as proof of apply-spam.
- Legal analysis (that is [01-T4](01-T4-CONSTRAINTS.md)).

## 7. Method

1. Open 5–8 Tier A sources before press ([01-T5](01-T5-SOURCES-PASS.md) will check this). Log them in [SOURCES.md](../SOURCES.md) as you go.
2. Q1: BFS / opendata.swiss (or equivalent). ≤5 bullets, each checkable. Skip decoration stats that cannot change a product or beachhead choice.
3. Q2: search for apply-volume, screening load, AI applications. If the trail is thin, write **weak** and say what a stronger source would look like.
4. Q3–Q4: one clear fact → one so what. Do not stack five unsourced “shifts.”
5. Q5: pick a trend that would change *this* product if reversed (e.g. apply noise collapses, or salary stays fully opaque). Fact → so what → **inference** if wrong.

---

## 8. Sub-questions — what “complete” means

### 01-T2-Q1 — Official stats

≤5 bullets. Each: what the official source says + URL + access date. Relevance to a CH marketplace in the so-what column, not in a sixth bullet of commentary.

### 01-T2-Q2 — Apply volume / unqualified / AI applications

What exists, how strong it is, seeker-side vs employer-side if you can tell them apart. “Weak / anecdotal / dated” is an acceptable answer if true.

### 01-T2-Q3 — Skills-based / structured requirements

What is changing (sourced) + **so what** for bidirectional matching (seeker constraints ↔ job requirements), not for a better keyword search box.

### 01-T2-Q4 — Salary transparency / benchmarks in CH

What you can source (law, surveys, portal features, official wage data). So what for **company salary ranges** and **seeker floors** on profiles. `unknown` beats a guessed median wage.

### 01-T2-Q5 — One more trend

Fact → so what → what we would do differently if the trend is wrong (**inference**). Must matter to matching, liquidity, trust, or go-to-market — not a random tech headline.

---

## 11. Common mistakes

- Trend list with no “so what.”
- Using the brief’s 40–50 applications / 3–6 months without a new source.
- Q1 as a data dump of every BFS table you opened.
- Q3 that only says “AI matching is the future.”
- Q4 inventing salary bands for a niche (that is T3/later, and still must be sourced).
