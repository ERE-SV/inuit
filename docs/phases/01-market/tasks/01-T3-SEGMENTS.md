## Why this exists

Gate 1 (Week 4) will choose a **niche × geography**. Before that, we need to notice how a few candidate segments **differ** — not pick a winner yet.

T3 is **comparison**: same industries you already looked at in T1, side by side, with one extra lens T1 often skips — **supply** (candidates, not just job openings).

---

## Our guideline

- **Compare 2–3 segments** — enough to see differences for Gate 1; not an exhaustive market map.
- **Reuse T1, don’t re-invent.** Demand-side numbers come from your T1 notes — cite them, don’t start a second spreadsheet.
- **Same three industries** unless you have a one-line reason to swap. Names in [docs/brief/03-gtm-niche.md](../../../brief/03-gtm-niche.md) are starting ideas, not findings.
- **Compare, don’t crown.** Two sentences on liquidity vs pain — not “our beachhead is X.”
- **Label weak proxies.** Portal hit counts and LinkedIn member counts are snapshots, not market size.
- **Take location seriously.** Nurses, trades, and other local-heavy roles can kill a CH-wide story.

High pain + no supply is a warning sign, not a wedge.

## What we're doing now

**Goal:** Put three candidate segments next to each other and notice differences — especially demand vs supply vs geography.

Example questions:

**Which three?**

- Which segments will you compare? Default = the **same industries** you used in T1 (e.g. IT/consultants, banking, healthcare, trades — pick three).
- If you substitute one, why?

**Demand (mostly from T1)**

- For each segment: openings, tightness, hiring pain, time-to-hire, fluctuation, wrong-hire cost — what did T1 already show?
- What is still `unknown`?

**Supply (the T3 add)**

- For each segment: graduates, seeker pools, workforce stats, network proxies — what can you find?
- What is honestly `unknown` after a search?

**Geography**

- Where is hiring concentrated for each segment (city, canton, remote)?
- What does that imply for Zurich-first vs CH-wide? (**inference** OK if labeled.)

**Liquidity vs pain (observation only)**

- Which segment looks strongest on **liquidity** (enough openings *and* seekers)?
- Which looks strongest on **pain / cost of bad hire**?
- If those are different segments, say so — do not force one winner.

You might sketch a comparison table: segment · demand · supply · geography · notes — not a required form.

**Start here**

1. Name your three segments (same as T1 unless you justify a swap).  
2. Pull demand-side cells from T1 — don’t invent new series.  
3. Hunt **supply** only for what's missing.  
4. Write two sentences: liquidity leader vs pain leader.  
5. Link from the worksheet.
