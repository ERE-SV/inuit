## Learn (orientation)

### What this phase is

A **lean brand constraint pack** so every later screen looks and sounds like the same company: promise, personality, tone, and visual rules that Figma must obey. Not a full logo book or marketing campaign. Builds on Gate 1–2 and Phase 02. Optional dump: [BRAND-PRINCIPLES.md](BRAND-PRINCIPLES.md).

### Branding vs marketing vs positioning

| Concept | What it is | Everyday analogy | What you do in *this* phase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Positioning** | Slot you want to own vs alternatives | Which shelf in the store | Promise line + who you are not |
| **Branding** | How that position *feels* (words, personality, visuals) | Store look, voice, name | Personality, tone, visuals, naming |
| **Marketing** | How you *reach* people | Ads, outreach | Mostly Phase 04 — brand sets the voice |

### Key terms

| Term | Meaning in plain language | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Promise line** | One sentence: what we deliver and for whom | Gate 3 locks this for UI copy |
| **Target group** | Who the product is for (seekers *and* companies) | Brand must work on both sides |
| **Buyer vs user** | Who pays / decides vs who clicks daily | Tone differs by role |
| **Jobs-to-be-done** | Progress a person wants | Personality must match those jobs |
| **Brand personality** | Human traits the company should feel like | Guides tone and visuals |
| **Tone do / don’t** | Concrete writing rules | Prevents HR-bashing or hype |
| **Visual principles** | Color, type, density, imagery — enough for Figma | Binding for Phase 06 |
| **Naming direction** | Options or criteria for the product name | Can stay open; principles still lock |

### Common mistakes

- Confusing a slogan campaign with positioning  
- Brand for “everyone in Switzerland” instead of the locked beachhead  
- Ignoring the company side  
- Treating “HR” as one person  
- Full brand books that block Gate 3  
- Vague visuals (“modern and clean”)
