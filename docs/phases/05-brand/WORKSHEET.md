## Learn (read before working)

### What this phase is

You write a **lean brand constraint pack** so every later screen looks and sounds like the same company. Brand here is not a full logo book or a marketing campaign plan — it is the promise, personality, tone, and visual rules that Figma must obey. You build on Gate 1–2 (beachhead + GTM) and Phase 02 positioning. Optional long write-up: [BRAND-PRINCIPLES.md](BRAND-PRINCIPLES.md).

### Branding vs marketing vs positioning (plain language)

| Concept | What it is | Everyday analogy | What you do in *this* phase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Positioning** | The slot you want to own in the buyer’s mind vs alternatives (“we are the X for Y, not the Z”) | Choosing which shelf you sit on in the store | One clear promise line + who you are not |
| **Branding** | How that position *feels* in words, personality, and visuals every time someone meets you | The store’s look, voice, and name | Personality, tone do/don’t, visual principles, naming direction |
| **Marketing** | How you *reach* people and persuade them to try you (channels, campaigns, content) | Ads, posts, sales outreach | Mostly Phase 04 GTM — brand only sets the voice those messages must use |

If positioning is wrong, pretty branding just makes the wrong promise look polished. If branding is vague, Figma and GTM invent different personalities.

### Key terms

| Term | Meaning in plain language | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Promise line** | One sentence: what we deliver and for whom | Gate 3 locks this for all UI copy |
| **Target group** | Who the product is for (seekers *and* companies) | Brand must work on both sides of the marketplace |
| **Buyer vs user** | Who pays / decides to buy vs who clicks daily | Tone for HR ≠ tone for a hiring manager ≠ tone for a board slide |
| **Jobs-to-be-done** | The progress a person wants (“get fewer bad applications,” “find a role without 50 cover letters”) | Personality must match those jobs, not generic “innovation” |
| **Brand personality** | Human traits the company should feel like (e.g. precise, calm, fair) | Guides tone and visuals |
| **Tone do / don’t** | Concrete writing rules | Prevents HR-bashing or hype that the brief forbids |
| **Visual principles** | Color, type, density, imagery rules — enough for Figma, not a full design system | Binding for Phase 06 |
| **Naming direction** | Options or criteria for the product name | Can stay open as options; principles still lock |

### Common beginner mistakes

- Confusing a slogan campaign (marketing) with positioning (category slot)  
- Writing brand for “everyone in Switzerland” instead of the locked beachhead  
- Ignoring the **company** side (brand that only speaks to seekers)  
- Treating “HR” as one person — buyer, champion, and user may differ  
- Full brand books / logo explorations that block Gate 3  
- Tone that bashes HR without evidence (see GTM messaging caution)  
- Visual rules too vague (“modern and clean”) for a designer to configure Figma
